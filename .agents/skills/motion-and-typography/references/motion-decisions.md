# Motion decisions

Choose motion from the meaning, and pace from the music and the reference's numbers, not from a general belief about what viewers can take. A viewer should know what to look at before a second element competes for attention. That is a rule about hierarchy, not about slowness: a 0.7 s median can be perfectly clear, and a 4 s hold can lose the viewer.

Nothing should sit dead for long. A stop is an event and gets planned. Hold when the held thing is doing work: being read, being demonstrated, or being the loudest moment in the film.

## How to decide

Four questions, in order. Then pick from the [motion grammar](motion-grammar.md).

1. **What does this moment mean?** Something falls, is corrected, adds up, gives way. The entrance and the exit follow from that verb. If you only have a noun, you have a slide.
2. **What is the music doing here?** A hit, a run of hits, a breath, a rise. The picture plays against it, and the film rests where the music rests.
3. **What crosses into the next shot?** An object, a line, a direction, a sound. If nothing does, make that decision on purpose.
4. **How much time do the words need?** Fast in, then readable. Short labels about 0.8 s, 0.3 s a word beyond that, 1.25 times for Cyrillic. If there is no room, use fewer words rather than less reading.

In a bespoke film this is the motion-director's job, written into the plan before code. In template mode it is decided per scene from the registered templates.

## Worked decisions

Good product example: show the actual control at readable scale and let the press produce the result, so that the state change is the cut. The button's press opens the next shot, and the benefit arrives inside that shot rather than as a caption. A device frame is secondary to the demonstrated interaction.

Good educational example: keep one comparison chart on screen while both alternatives are discussed, and reveal the series or callout as the narration reaches it. Title, axis units and uncertainty stay visible. This is a long shot that keeps changing, and that is why it holds: the same chart gives a new fact each time. Do not cut merely to cut, and do not hold merely to feel calm.

Good kinetic example: one line of light survives four cuts, from a blade's edge to the cut through a tomato to the word's baseline. Shot lengths run from 5 to 109 frames. The longest shot sits where the bass drops out of the track, and a tick in the music flattens the edge a section at a time. It is a plan, not yet a proven film.

Failure: five successive numbers spring in because the library supports spring. The viewer cannot remember the baseline or tell which number proves the claim. Use one decisive value with source and context, or a labelled comparison.

Failure: a word held still on an orange fill for 4.2 s at the film's turn, with the entrance every other word had, and nothing crossing out of it. Our own Showcase. A title card where the loudest moment should be.

Failure: fewer scenes held longer because that felt confident. Measured against ten references it was 3.9 s against 0.2 to 2.2 s, and the user called it weak in dynamics. Whether a long take is right depends on the film, so measure first.

## Typography order

Short headline, then evidence or visual, then optional detail. In template mode: `text.word-reveal` suits speech-order emphasis, `text.line-reveal` suits logical steps, `text.clip-reveal` suits a composed editorial phrase, and `text.count-up` is for a verified change with an intelligible unit. Avoid typewriter for ordinary narration if it delays comprehension. In a bespoke film, type is a performer: see the [grammar's section](motion-grammar.md#type-as-performer).

An audio cue should reinforce an actual change, not fire on every word. Endings can resolve, recap, request one action or loop; educational content does not need a sales call to action.

## Review rubric

Clear focal point at every moment, motivated reveal, proof legible at playback size, complete thought before exit, no conflict between a visual and an audio boundary, the same hierarchy in vertical, square and landscape. Then, from a strip of consecutive frames and not from a contact sheet, which hides motion: does each boundary carry, does each landing settle and leave, is anything frozen that was not planned, and is any shot a dead hold? Record observed failures and targeted fixes, not a generic "looks good".
