# Caption decisions

Good short-form example: “Three mistakes cost us a week.” Group the sentence into readable units; accent “three mistakes” when it is the hook. Do not highlight every word with equal urgency. The visual demonstration supplies the proof.

Good education example: show the whole short definition while the teacher explains it; highlight the term once and hold through a pause. Leave diagram labels readable. Caption position may change with layout, but source timing remains tied to the same speech.

Failure: one-word captions flash at high cadence while the shot contains a dense chart. Eye movement between chart and text prevents either from being understood. Use a calm phrase or simplify the visual argument.

Grouping follows punctuation, semantics and breath. Maximum words is a layout ceiling, not a linguistic rule. Never split a name, number with unit or negation from the statement it changes. A pause may hold a phrase without marking an unspoken word active. Hide after the last timed word unless an intentional editorial hold is explicitly authored.

Localization alternatives: two-line reflow, shorter spoken rewrite approved for meaning, separate localized video, or a bilingual layout developed and previewed deliberately. Avoid reducing type below a readable token just to preserve the original line break.

Review: unchanged factual meaning, exact word starts/ends, correct source offset after trim, no accumulated drift, safe-zone coverage in all requested formats, legible accent/outline, no hidden changes to the presenter audio. Human review remains necessary for idioms and educational correctness.
