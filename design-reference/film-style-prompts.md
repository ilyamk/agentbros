# Film style prompts

A style prompt fixes what stays true for every film in one medium (look, colour logic, type, motion, camera vocabulary, sound palette, native moves, pitfalls) and leaves story, shots and timing to the film. Use it when an idea calls for a medium (ink, print, tape, a whiteboard, a circuit board) rather than our default flat product look: the design-director writes one for the film's look, and the concept-director can borrow a native move as an idea's device.

Adapted from [lemo-opuscar](https://github.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar/tree/9eb7739a0fca4c6990fe966cb8b87b1930f7193a) by LemoLab (code and docs MIT; demo films CC BY 4.0, not imported). The full prompt for any style below is at `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar/9eb7739a0fca4c6990fe966cb8b87b1930f7193a/styles/<slug>/STYLE.md`; read it for the medium's rules, never for its demo. ## Writing one

Eleven fixed sections, 6–11 KB. Rules, not history; no story, arc, shot table, BPM, key or end card (those belong to the film's own plan).

1. **Essence, and what it is not**: 3–5 traits that make a frame read as this style at a glance, then the styles it is confused with and the line between them.
2. **Materials and rendering**: the physical medium and how code imitates it; which effects are anchored to the world and which live in screen space.
3. **Colour logic**: rules, not a palette (how many hues, the neutral ground, what the accent is reserved for, what may never be coloured). One example palette at most.
4. **Type and subtitles**: licence-free faces and their roles, size ranges, how captions belong to the world (inscription, label, HUD plate).
5. **Motion quality**: frame rate and stepping, easing families, weight, what boils, how things appear and leave, what never moves.
6. **Camera grammar**: a table of moves, what each expresses and what it can serve. No row names the move for the opening, the peak or the end.
7. **Sound palette**: instruments and timbres, foley materials (what this matter sounds like), ambience, how silence is cut, mix and voice character. No score arc, BPM or key.
8. **Native moves**: moments only this medium can do, each with how to build it and "fits content like" three examples unlike the reference.
9. **Pitfalls of the medium**, each with its fix.
10. **Engine**: which of our blocks or files hold the recipes.
11. **Variation space**: what each film decides, then three structures, three openings and three endings, all far from any reference.

The first idea a style suggests is usually its cliché: choose the opening, the ending and the score's shape from the topic.

## The 66 styles

One line each: essence, then native moves. Pick by the content's material, not by the name.

### Edit & Documentary

- **Caption News** (`caption-news`): Social-video news that reads with the sound off. Moves: Frame-4 hook; Chunk slam with one yellow keyword; Proportion by darkness; The document says it; Freeze and question; Ledger split; Lit grid; Punch and circle; Map to ground.
- **1920s Newsreel** (`newsreel`): A sound newsreel of the late 1920s, cut from real archive material: a searchlight-and-globe ident with a brass fanfare, a newspaper that spins into the lens, a fast and cheerful announcer on an optical soundtrack… Moves: The ident ritual; The spinning paper; The map spread; Grid deal → flip-book; Document underline; Grease-pencil freeze; Split-screen duel → push-over; Needle drop; Band stop + button.
- **Live Countdown Split-screen** (`countdown`): An edit style, not a look. Moves: Many into one; Clock inversion; Control strip; The grid fills on the count; Sync badge; Planned vs heard checklist; Second countdown; Event flag; Punch-in on a call; Document by rows.
- **Time-lapse Symphony** (`city-symphony`): A city film cut like a piece of minimalist music: time-lapse turns traffic, crowds and clouds into rivers of light, the cuts get denser with the score's subdivisions, one thing is slowed almost to a stop…
- **Visual Investigation** (`visual-investigation`): A cold investigation desk: footage, photographs, documents, sound and maps from one real event, laid out on one shared clock so they check each other. Moves: Freeze, circle, magnify; Hard sync point; Three-column sync grid; Readouts change scale; Rewind cold open; Telegrams on the clock; Data chart with a playhead; Map → plan; Evidence → model links; Then vs. record; Known / unresolved; Dots for people; Evidence map.
- **Constructivist Montage** (`kino-montage`): Real archive footage, photographs and documents, printed as one ink-and-paper poster with a single red, and cut on a machine beat. Moves: The document is marked, not quoted; The document becomes the chart; A + B → idea; Count the many; People build the structure; Silence, then the heaviest hit.
- **Sports Telestrator** (`telestrator`): The TV football analysis segment: play the clip at full speed, rewind, freeze it, and draw on the broadcast picture — rings under players' feet, marker arrows for the ball, a red patch for the space nobody covered — then play on… Moves: Freeze and draw; Tracked ring; Frame-counting stopwatch; Score-bar flip on the goal frame; Packaging over watermarks; Dual-angle freeze; Perspective flatten into a shot map; Data card; Commentary transcript.
- **Broadcast News Package** (`news-package`): A television newsroom's on-air package, built entirely in code: a sting that becomes the channel bug, a rundown of today's stories that opens and closes the bulletin, a lower-third strap that never leaves the bottom of the frame… Moves: Sting becomes the bug; Strap tab = category; Rundown, then ticked off; Spec card; Strap that never leaves; Split-flap before/after; Breaking the threshold; Date in the eyebrow; City sends the letters; Quote card + verdict stamp.

### Evidence Films

- **Satellite Briefing** (`satellite-briefing`): Real satellite imagery of a real place, graded down to an analyst's grey, with thin white measurement lines drawn on the ground: a lock-on reticle, outlines, a range line, one reading. Moves: The map against the photograph; Time rings in a locked frame; The range line; The dive and the climb; Colour returns where the story turns; The reticle becomes the frame.
- **Engineering Briefing Board** (`briefing-board`): One real whiteboard wall, filmed: window light on enamel, ghosts of erased marker, frame and tray. Moves: The whole-wall reveal; The subject passes through the answer; Ride the dimension line; Projection lines; Magnets as countable things; Erase to a ghost; The stamp; The physical answer; Leader to the verdict.
- **LiDAR Survey** (`lidar-survey`): Real airborne laser points of a real place, floating in the dark as a measured cloud; a scan line or a cutting plane passes through them and the points condense into a solid, raking-lit surface… Moves: Photo to points; The canopy peel; Points condense; The section; The window; The regrowth.
- **Projection on White Model** (`white-model`): A pure white massing model of a real place stands on a plinth in a dark room, and a projector paints data onto it as light: one layer at a time, falling on the surfaces that face it, spilling a little past the plate. Moves: Lights out, lamp on; The hole; Layers by time; The rising level; The sun clock; The pick-out; Lights up.
- **Data Physicalization** (`data-physical`): Real statistics built as real objects: every unit is a countable piece of material (a plate, a block, a coin, a bar) stacked to exact scale on a crafted base, shot like a product in a studio — soft key, long shadows… Moves: The unit close-up; The paired climb; The feed; Chart to installation; The last object.
- **Exploded Teardown** (`teardown`): A real product, built from its own engineering files (CAD, PCB layout, bill of materials), lit like a studio packshot and taken apart in front of the camera: the layers lift on one axis, the camera travels between them… Moves: The wave lift; Inside the gap; The leader that lands on the part; Dimensions drawn on the thing; One real motion; Lights on inside.
- **Microscope Story** (`microscope`): Real, licence-clean microscopy of a real specimen, seen as it is, and one continuous change of scale that carries the viewer from the glass slide in the hand to a single cell. Moves: The dive; Counting the colour; Two circles, one verdict; The focal sweep and the ring; The climb back.
- **Archive Light Table** (`archive-lighttable`): Real archive objects (glass negatives, slides, letters, telegrams, clippings, drawings) lie on a cool, backlit light table. Moves: Negative to positive under the loupe; The line lights up; Into the photograph; Measuring on the photograph; Two records, held side by side; The table becomes the index.

### Hand-drawn & Painting

- **Crayon Picture Book** (`crayon-book`): A picture-book page drawn live in wax crayon on toothy paper, with a watercolour wash that reveals what the crayon was hiding. Moves: Wax resist; Drawn live; Colouring is an action; Boil; Child logic; It is a book.
- **Watercolor Brush** (`watercolor`): A hand-painted journal page that paints itself: translucent brush strokes with dry-brush flying white on warm cotton paper, hand-written notes beside what is painted, nothing drawn with a vector line. Moves: Painting-in as reveal; Burst; Palette as data; Specimen + note; Mist erase; Map in time; Painted transformation.
- **Chinese Ink Wash** (`ink-wash`): Xieyi ink painting on rice paper, animated: blank paper is space, one brushstroke is a mountain, a wave, a gesture or a cut. Moves: One stroke is everything; Emptiness is matter; Brush = gesture = action; Flying white (飞白); Ink bloom transition; The handscroll; Splitting a mass.
- **Impasto Oil Painting** (`impasto`): Thick oil paint laid with a palette knife: big, flat, sharp-edged planes of colour, raked by light so every ridge of paint shows. Moves: Colour re-laid by the knife; The one coloured thing; Motion paints the picture; Raking light; Paint-over transition; Sound made visible.
- **One-line Drawing** (`one-line`): A single ink line that never leaves the paper. Moves: The pen never lifts; The line is time; Transformation; Stopping is an event; Reverse-designed scale reveal; Handoff; Line as graph.
- **Whiteboard Explainer** (`whiteboard`): An explainer drawn live on one glossy dry-erase board: single-line handwriting that is actually *written* stroke by stroke, floating markers with no hand, magnets that can move, an eraser that can rewind… Moves: Writing in stroke order; The pen leads the camera; Magnets; The eraser rewinds; Two pens at once; Ghosts of the old lesson; The full-board view.
- **Urban Sketch · Pen & Wash** (`urban-sketch`): A sketchbook page drawn on location: fine sepia fineliner lines that wobble, overshoot and never quite close, with loose transparent watercolour dropped on top. Moves: Line before colour; Colour as arrival; The one coloured thing; The page as frame; Carrot people.
- **Chalkboard** (`chalkboard`): A real classroom blackboard on which chalk writes itself, stroke by stroke in true stroke order, with no hand, no chalk stick and no eraser in sight. Moves: Write, wipe, rewrite in place; Tracked component in accent chalk; Sliding panel reveal; A line that sews two boards; Drying wet streak; Second hand; Chalk as a measuring tool.

### East Asian Traditions

- **Shadow Puppetry** (`shadow-puppet`): Chinese shadow theatre: translucent dyed-hide puppets, carved like lace, pressed against a cotton screen and lit from behind by oil lamps. Moves: The light is the world; Off the screen = big and blurry; Go behind the screen; Carved and translucent; Hand-held; Piece swap; Strip lift-off.
- **Ukiyo-e** (`ukiyoe`): Edo-period woodblock prints brought to life: flat colour blocks, carved ink lines, bokashi skies, paper you can feel, and a camera that moves over the print like an unrolling handscroll. Moves: A series of views; The handscroll; Printing, block by block; Paper is the white; The frame is an object; Alternate impressions; The triptych.
- **Red Paper-cut** (`papercut-red`): Chinese window-flower paper-cuts brought to life: jointed red cut-outs on paper grounds, where the cut *is* the drawing and the holes are where the light gets in. Moves: Fold – cut – unfold; Holes are light; Symmetry replicates; Scraps have a future; Everything is one flat sheet; Mirrored pair.
- **Paper-cut Lightbox** (`paper-lantern`): A layered paper-cut shadow box (纸雕灯) lit from behind: 6–9 sheets of cut card stacked in a shallow box, a warm light glowing through the paper, windows and moons burning through holes… Moves: The lamp turns on; Two windows, one light; Depth as distance; Paper that writes itself; The box as keepsake; Light burst from an opened thing; Silhouette in a window.

### Print & Printmaking

- **Risograph Print** (`risograph`): Animation that looks printed, frame by frame, on a stencil duplicator: two or three translucent spot inks overprinted on warm paper, halftone dots, ink grain, and plates that never quite line up. Moves: Plates as narrative; Two inks, a third where they meet; Overprint transition; Registration as rhythm and feeling; Reflections as separated plates; Knockout reveal; Halftone scale; It is a sheet of paper.
- **Halftone Dossier** (`halftone-dossier`): A retro print case file: cream archive paper, overprinted halftone dots in a few flat inks, huge heavy headlines with misregistered shadows, rubber stamps that slam, and thick-outlined characters. Moves: The accusation frame; Numbered exhibits; The stamp; The mugshot; Evidence board; Dot density = emotion; Redaction.
- **Woodcut Print** (`woodcut`): A relief print that carves itself: every frame is a black wood block, and light only exists where a knife has cut it away. Moves: Light is carved; Carving = time; Mirrored block → true print; Stab cuts as texture; The one colour plate spreads and withdraws; Filter a sculpt or a frame; Wordless-novel sequencing.
- **Copperplate Engraving** (`engraving`): An intaglio plate that engraves itself: a burin cuts swelling lines into copper, a proof is pulled, and the figure builds up — outline, hatching, cross-hatching — until engraved lettering… Moves: The cut; The three passes; Magnification roundel; The proof; States of the plate; Hand colouring; Natural size; The finished plate.
- **Silkscreen Travel Poster** (`silkscreen-poster`): Opaque, hard-edged flat inks pulled one colour at a time through a screen: 4–6 inks per poster, stepped colour bands instead of gradients, thin paper-white gaps where the registration slips. Moves: One ink, one beat; Split fountain; Palette re-ink; Registration slip → snap; Paper as a colour; The route as a printed layer; The series wall.
- **Printed Long Take** (`printed-long-take`): A three-dimensional world rendered as a scratchboard print, ink and paper with one red, crossed by one unbroken camera move. Moves: The sky carved open; One red waking; Through the steam; The same place printed again; Words cut into the world; A plate of type in the sky.

### Graphic & Type

- **Swiss Motion Graphics** (`swiss-motion`): International Typographic Style in motion: a modular grid, one typeface, one signal colour, and movement as exact as a printing press. Moves: The grid means something; Snap = beat; Programme; Extreme scale contrast; The exception; Knife cut; Re-flow.
- **60s Spy Title Sequence** (`spy-titles`): A cut-paper, four-ink opening title sequence for a film that never existed: silhouettes move through sets built out of the credits themselves, every brass stab is a cut, and a title is assembled from the pieces. Moves: Type is the set; Credits are rhythm; Graphic match cuts; The diagonal grid; Silhouette puppets; Stop-time gag; The object of the chase is the title.
- **Art Deco** (`art-deco`): Black lacquer, engraved gold line and airbrushed geometry: a 1930s poster that moves. Moves: Counted light; Threshold doors; Machine kaleidoscope; Dials carry the plot; The architectural cutaway; Sheen sweep / draw-on; The one colour.
- **Blueprint** (`blueprint`): Engineering drawings that come alive on a sheet of cyanotype paper: white technical line work on Prussian blue, drawn in drafting order, with annotations that move. Moves: Drafting order; Live annotations; Fig. numbers as transitions; Projection as camera; A symbol made real; Cyanotype and water; The title block as story.
- **Stained Glass** (`stained-glass`): A medieval stained-glass window seen from inside a dark stone hall, alive only where the sun touches it: light is the clock, the camera and the story. Moves: Light is time, and the camera; Glass projects onto stone; Glass cracks, lead mends; A window holds its own light; The window as a diagram.
- **Pictogram Motion** (`pictogram-motion`): A beat-locked film built from three things only: a square-grid "core graphic" of circles, half-discs and tiny patterned cells whose rows slide past each other; geometric pictograms (objects, vehicles, weather, signs… Moves: One card = one item; Chapter = colour; One verb per item; Pace ladder run; Long take down a track; Count-up slam; Icon wall.
- **ASCII / CRT Terminal** (`ascii-crt`): Everything on screen is a real character on a monospace grid, lit by the phosphor of an old monochrome CRT. Moves: The picture is made of words; Font size = camera; Typing rhythm = editing rhythm; CRT physics; A single colour change; The log file.

### Information & Keynote

- **Data Storytelling** (`dataviz`): A chart that tells a story: real data, drawn mark by mark on cream paper by a red–blue pencil, with handwritten notes pinned to the numbers. Moves: One mark = one fact; Axes grow with the story; Pinned annotations; Breaking the frame; Encoding morph; Scale switch; The empty next cell.
- **Isometric Infographic** (`iso-infographic`): A system diagram comes alive: an isometric diorama of flat three-tone blocks, thin ink labels and Isotype numbers, where things open up to show their insides and the numbers are drawn, not stated. Moves: Cutaway; Isotype number; Focus + context; Frame within the frame; Scale reveal; Tracked subject; Strata cross-section.
- **Dark Tech Keynote** (`dark-keynote`): A software launch film where the interface itself is the star: near-black gradient, hairline grid, soft light, one brand accent, and UI that moves with snap-grid precision. Moves: Generated accumulation; The snap grid; The living element; Freeze; Light reveal; Number from glyph noise; Type streams; Put-away.
- **Living Screencast** (`living-screencast`): A product walkthrough that looks like a real screen recording, where a low-resolution mascot lives inside a high-resolution interface and acts out what the software is doing. Moves: Match-cut origin; Diegetic title; Moving floor; Time-lapse with a tag; Spotlight freeze; The film changes state; Cell division; Cursor and mascot as characters; Karaoke-ball words.
- **Sci-fi Hologram HUD** (`hologram-hud`): An object is scanned into a glowing wireframe. Moves: Scan growth = "this is real data."; Target lock = "look here now."; Local explode = "why the number is true."; Rolling digits = the payoff beat; Chip accumulation = reading time; Loupe = two scales at once; Solid hologram + beam = "the whole is ready.".
- **Transit Map** (`transit-map`): A metro schematic printed on a physical sheet: lines held to eight directions, white stops, black-outlined interchange capsules, water as a flat blue band. Moves: The pen tip; The interchange that falls; The tunnel under the water; Time lines; Levels of detail; You are here; The odometer; The plate by the lens.
- **Isometric Picture Book** (`isometric-picture-book`): A calm explainer of an everyday rule, set in one real place drawn in true isometric with flat colour: small faceless people seen from behind do the thing in order, while serif type lands on the ground, character by character… Moves: Floor type; Domino stand-up; Lid off; Order lighting; Order arrows; Weave fill; Bars on the ground; People as the sample; Stand here.
- **Circuit Board** (`circuit-board`): The subject is drawn as a printed circuit board seen from straight above: the world is copper under green solder mask, routes are traces, places are pads, words are white silkscreen, and what flows (power, water, signals… Moves: Flow becomes current; Pulse-path explainer; The map is a board; Detail callout; Nested boards; Matched step-down; Timestamp tags.

- **HD-2D** (`hd-2d`): Hand-made pixel sprites standing like paper cut-outs inside a lit, fogged, fully 3D diorama, shot from a high three-quarter angle through a tilt-shift lens: a toy world where every lamp throws a real shadow. - **Microgame Frenzy** (`microgame`): A rapid-fire party-game film: one command word, a few seconds, one action. Moves: Style switch per game; Command word; Lives as UI; SPEED UP; Failure becomes the solution; Boss mixes the rules; Instant replay.

### Cinema & Eras

- **Liminal Found Footage** (`backrooms`): Camcorder tapes recovered from empty, endless, fluorescent-lit spaces. Moves: Autofocus / auto-exposure hunting; Tape tracking damage; The OSD is plot; Rules as text in frame; Emptiness and repetition; The thing that is almost human.
