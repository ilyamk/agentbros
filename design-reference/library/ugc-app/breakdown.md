# UGC App: method breakdown

17 s, 1920x1080, 30 fps. For study only. The detector found no voice. I did not hear the sound; conclusions about it are drawn from the measurements.

**Important:** this is not a UGC film. It is a motion film in which UGC clips serve as material. The file contains only the problem statement: there is no product and no answer, and the last frame breaks off mid-motion. It may be part of a longer film or a teaser.

## Idea

Show the problem through scale: the topic is everywhere, the people are beyond counting, the work takes hundreds of hours. Then one question and a full stop.

## Hook (0–2.9 s)

In a white void a search bar grows, and a phrase is slowly typed into it. Then a sharp break in speed: eight different pages in 0.8 s, 2–4 frames per page, and on each one the same phrase is highlighted in yellow at the same point in the frame. The gaze stays still, the world changes around it. No words saying "it's everywhere" are needed.

## Structure

| Time | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2.1 | Typing the query | Set up the topic |
| 2.1–2.9 | Eight pages with the highlight | Prove the topic is everywhere |
| 2.9–4.5 | Lips open out into a person, a column of clips | Give it a face |
| 4.5–6.9 | The column becomes a wall of dozens of clips | Too many faces |
| 6.9–13.1 | Three figures with sources over dark shots | The cost in hours |
| 13.1–17 | Big and small words: a six-word question about the best way, in four lines | The question |

## Sound and editing

The tempo is shown as 128 BPM at a low confidence of 0.46. Cuts on the beat: 46% against a chance 34%, almost nothing. On peaks: 92% against a chance 71%, but on only 13 cuts and with dense peaks, so it is only a hint. The page cuts come every 0.1 s and cannot sit on individual hits.

Another measurement, which I made separately from loudness, proved more precise. The sound swells sharply at about 4.2 s, and after that an uneven pattern of bursts repeats: after 0.7, 0.9 and 1.4 s, the whole cycle about 3.7 s. That is exactly 8 beats at 128 BPM, so the tempo may be right. Four of the five main picture changes land on a burst to within 0.1 s: 6.9, 11.4, 13.07 and 15.07 s. By chance such a hit happens in about 24% of cases. But I chose these five changes after already seeing the bursts, so this is a guess backed by data, not proof. What exactly sounds at these moments, I do not know.

The rhythm here lies in the changes of speed, not in a grid: a long shot, a flash, continuous motion, calm reading, four words.

The detector found 14 shots and was badly wrong in both directions: it missed 4 of the 8 pages and all the fast background changes behind the text, yet it cut the continuous move from the person to the wall. The "match" label is wrong here: the background changes, and the text simply holds.

## Signature moves

1. **Cuts locked on the highlight (2.1–2.9 s).** Everything changes except one bright point. The eye holds on to it, and so this speed reads as one thought rather than noise. Each page needs to be convincing for only 3 frames.
2. **From lips to crowd in one move (2.9–6.9 s).** A crop on the lips opens out into a person in 6 frames, the column moves. From 4.5 s white blurred tiles appear at the sides and turn into clips from the centre outwards, the wall pulls back from 6 to about 16 columns. One person physically becomes a statistic.
3. **A centred phrase over dark cuts (6.9–13.1 s).** The line holds at one height and builds word by word: the new word is yellow and cools to white over 9 frames, while the earlier ones shift left to keep the line centred. A yellow plate first opens empty, then fills, later jumps to the figure. A source is typed at the bottom. The background, at about 25% brightness, changes every 2–3 frames at first, then less often. The picture stays alive, yet it can be read calmly.
4. **Size as emphasis (13.1–17 s).** Big words float through the frame and are in focus only near the centre. Then a tiny "there was a" for 0.8 s, and the biggest word, "better", with a blue glow. The longest pause is on the smallest words, so the viewer waits.

## What we can and cannot do

**We can in Remotion:** the typing, the highlight lock, the centred phrase with the plate, the big words. Small primitives need writing: typing with a cursor, alignment to the highlight, colour cooling, blur by position in the frame, text glow.

**Limited by material:** the wall of real people. Faces must not be generated and passed off as evidence; clips with consent are needed. The background shots must be licensed or filmed. Without real clips this scene cannot honestly be made, only replaced with a different idea.

**Not sure:** the blue light at the end. It is visible for only a few frames, and we do not know how it should develop further. A suitable shader still has to be found.

**Must not be carried over:** their brand, text, figures, clips, pages and music. The figures in the new film must be real and sourced.
